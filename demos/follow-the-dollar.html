One dollar, from budget line to proof

A town budgets $120,000 for winter road maintenance and hires a plowing contractor. Here is every step that money takes through Civic-Chain, and the record each step leaves behind for anyone to check.

    Transparency Ledger

    The budget is published

    Finance uploads the adopted budget straight from the accounting system's export. Accounting checks confirm it balances, and the winter roads line goes public at $120,000.

    $0 committed · $0 paid of $120,000

  2. 2
    ProcurementOptional: residents vote first

    The work goes out to bid

    Staff build the solicitation from a template, with compliance requirements suggested from state rules. Approved vendors are emailed automatically, and the bid appears on the public listing.

    ProofPublic notice with a printable affidavit of posting
  3. 3
    Procurement

    Bids are sealed, then scored blind

    Four contractors bid, each proposal encrypted on the vendor's own device. Bids open in a witnessed ceremony. Evaluators score anonymously, and prices stay sealed until technical scores are locked.

    ProofOpening, scores and every action kept in the activity log

    The contractor is paid

    The contractor claims the first milestone and staff approve it: $16,000 by ACH, tracked to completion. At 3 a.m. during a storm, the crew's contract card buys $94.18 of fuel, inside the card's limit.

    The spending is published

    Card spending publishes to the ledger daily with no manual upload. At month end, the general ledger export publishes after the privacy scan and accounting checks.

    Public verification

    A resident checks it

    A resident opens the portal without an account and sees $48,000 committed and $16,094.18 paid against the $120,000 line. On the same verification page, they confirm the award and the spending match what was published.

  • Contractor

    Steps 3–5

  • Residents

    Steps 2, 7

  • Council and auditors

    Review the award and pull the contract's audit package.

    Steps 4, 7

What a town gives up with only one

Procurement alone

Steps 2 to 5 are provable: how the contractor was chosen and what they were paid. But the budget and the town's wider spending have to be published some other way, so residents can't see the contract against the line it draws from.

Transparency Ledger alone

Steps 1, 6 and 7 are provable: what was budgeted and what was spent. But how the contractor was chosen lives outside the record, and contract commitments have to be uploaded by hand.
